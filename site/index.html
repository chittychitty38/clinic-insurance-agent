Payer

The insurer

Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Medicare and others report coverage and cost-sharing.

  1. Find the chartName first, then date of birth if several patients share it, then the card itself. Two Jane Does share a birthday.
  2. Understand the insurerSpoken names map to payer IDs: "Signa" is Cigna, "United" is UnitedHealthcare. If it's ambiguous, Riley asks.
  3. Get the subscriber rightA student on a parent's plan is sent as a dependent under the policyholder, using the ID on file.
  4. Ask the payerA real-time 270 through Stedi, with one retry if the payer is down and a 12-second timeout.
  5. Read the 271 in codeActive or not, HMO, Medicare, dental-only, the right copay, coinsurance and deductible.
  6. Hand back the wordsThe server returns the exact sentence to say and the next step. Claude says it and keeps the conversation going.
The design decision that matters

The language model never decides a fact

A payer's response can list 40 different benefit lines. Asking a model to pick the right copay from that, live on a call, is how you quote a patient the wrong number. So the work is split, and each layer can only fail in its own way.

Claude, in Vapi

The conversation

One question at a time, reading member IDs back, tone, off-topic questions, eye emergencies (sends them to 911).

Can only be wrong in phrasing. Told to say the server's sentence and never state a number that isn't in a tool result.

eligibility/interpret.ts

Reading the 271

Active or inactive, plan dates, HMO and referrals, Medicare, dental-only, the visit copay, coinsurance, deductible.

Deterministic, unit-tested, and it refuses to guess.

eligibility/script.ts

The words

The exact sentence for each outcome and what Riley does next, like a callback, self-pay price or asking for the card again.

Reviewed copy, versioned in git like any other UI text.

payers.ts

Our contracts

Which insurers the clinic is in network with, and for which networks. The 271 can't tell you that.

Clinic configuration the practice manager owns.

What "accepted" means

accepted = clinic contract with the payer × coverage active today

Eligibility isn't acceptance. A Kaiser member with active coverage still isn't covered here, and a UnitedHealthcare member can be on a plan whose network leaves the clinic out. Riley only says yes when both sides agree, and says why when they don't.

What real payer data taught us

The first run passed 16 of 28. Every miss was a rule.

Documentation describes the 271. Real responses are messier. These are the cases that would have put a wrong number in a patient's ear.

"PROVIDER IS OUT NETWORK FOR MEMBER"

We take UnitedHealthcare. Her plan doesn't take us.

The payer says this member's plan lists the office as out of network. A naive agent says "you're covered." Riley says the plan lists us as out of network.

QUALIFIED_MEDICARE_BENEFICIARY

The Medicare patient who owes $0

0% coinsurance looked like a bug until the insurance type explained it: Medicaid pays her Medicare cost-sharing, and she can't be billed for it.

SPECIALIST, PAR NETWORK

Anthem lists two specialist copays

$30 on the EPO network, $45 on PAR. The 271 can't say which network the clinic is in, so that's clinic config, and Riley quotes $45.

coverageLevel: FAMILY

Aetna's copay hid behind a label

The $15 specialist copay is tagged FAMILY. That describes the plan, not the amount. Filtering it out silently lost the copay.

place of service 99

Cigna's $10 trap

The $10 copays were telehealth-vendor and "client specific network" lines. The real specialist copay is $20, filed under place of service "other."

payer ID 62308

A dental card with a medical payer ID

Cigna dental uses the same payer ID as Cigna medical, so dental-only plans are detected from the plan itself, and Riley asks for the medical card.

planDateInformation

Active, with 2024 plan dates

Sandbox mocks are frozen in time. In production, "active" with an expired plan date is a contradiction, and Riley escalates to a human instead of answering.

HTTP 429 + AAA 42

Errors don't always look like errors

A payer outage can arrive as a rate-limit status. The client keys off the 271's error codes, not the HTTP status, and retries once.

When the answer isn't yes

"No" and "I'm not sure" are part of the product

Most calls that matter are the hard ones. Riley never leaves a caller with a dead end, and never guesses to avoid one.

Not accepted

  • Confirms the coverage is real and active, so the caller knows their insurance works, just not here.
  • Explains why in one sentence (Kaiser plans only cover Kaiser doctors).
  • Offers the self-pay price and the member-services route to an in-network doctor.
  • Books a front-desk callback into the staff queue.
  • Never quotes a payer dollar amount on a plan we don't take. That's a test.

Payer errors

  • AAA 42 payer down: one automatic retry, then a callback.
  • AAA 72 / 73 / 75 ID, name or member not found: asks once to re-read the card or for the policyholder, then a callback.
  • AAA 43 / 79 our own setup rejected: tells the caller it isn't their fault and flags it for staff.
  • Inactive coverage: asks for a newer card before anything else.
  • Eye emergency mentioned: stops and sends them to 911 or the ER.
Roadmap

What it needs before real patients

This build proves the hard part: turning a payer response into a correct sentence. Production is mostly integration and compliance.

Verify identity

Name, date of birth and caller ID before discussing anything protected. The brief waived it for this version.

HIPAA end to end

BAAs with Vapi (HIPAA mode, no stored recordings), Stedi, the model provider and hosting. Call log moves to an encrypted database.

The real chart

Read patients and insurance on file from the practice-management system, like ModMed or Nextech, instead of a demo list.

Richer checks

Request office-visit (98) and vision (AL) service types alongside 30, add VSP and EyeMed for routine exams, and Medicare ID lookup for patients without their card.

Network per plan

Contract status per product, not per payer, since one insurer can sell plans on several networks.

Front-desk loop

Live transfer during hours, callbacks as tasks in the EHR, and alerts when our own setup is the problem.
